## Budget benchmarks

Figures used on the playbook page, with sources, so they can be checked.

| Figure | Value | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marketing spend, private B2B SaaS | 8% of revenue, median. Equity-backed companies spend roughly 2x bootstrapped. | SaaS Capital, 2026 Spending Benchmarks for Private B2B SaaS Companies (1,000+ companies surveyed) |
| Marketing spend, cross-industry | 7.8% of revenue | Gartner 2026 CMO Spend Survey (n=401, mostly $1B+ revenue). Directional only, sample skews enterprise. |
| M&A integration cost, technology / media / telecom | Above 5.6% of the acquired company's revenue, median. Rebranding named as a cost driver, not separately quantified. | EY-Parthenon, 2024. 236 deals of $500M+ closed 2010–2023. |
| Brand as a share of marketing budget | No reliable public benchmark. | — |
| Brand cost as a share of deal value | No credible published benchmark exists. Do not use this framing. | — |

Studio Parkside recommendations, not benchmarks: 10–15% of the marketing budget as a standing brand line, and 5–20% of a deal's integration budget for brand and communication depending on whether the name changes.

## Why the architecture matters

Figures carried over from the July strategy work. Sources are named but have not been re-verified against primary documents.

- **McKinsey:** strong-brand B2B companies see EBIT margins about 20% higher than weak-brand peers.
- **Kantar BrandZ:** strong brands outperformed the S&P 500 by 82 points over two decades, and fell less and recovered faster through both the 2008 and 2020 downturns.
- **LinkedIn:** companies with strong employer brands see up to 43% lower cost per hire.
- **Kantar:** brands that improved pricing power added 67% to brand value over four years, roughly twice the gain of everyone else.
