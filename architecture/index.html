01

About this playbook.

This is our brand playbook. It covers how our brands are organized, how we decide where something new belongs, and what happens once a decision is made.

It is written for anyone here. You do not need to work in marketing to use it. If you have ever been asked how the brands relate, or what happens when we buy a company, the answer is on this page.

It gives us the criteria and the order of operations before a decision gets made, so the thinking is visible to everyone rather than arriving as an announcement.

The decision framework, in short.

Every brand question runs through the same three moves. That is true for a company we might buy, for something we built ourselves, and for a brand that has been in the house for years.

1
A brand question comes up.

An acquisition, a launch, or something material changing around a brand we already run. Any name in the house, at any time.

2
Work through the inputs.

Eight of them, numbered in section 03, starting with the people who own what the decision touches. No skipping, no scoring.

3
Decide, then record it.

The answer is Brand, Product, or Feature. It goes into the playbook file with the date and the reasoning, then gets communicated in order.

The checklist at the end of section 03 confirms nothing got skipped. Every box checked means the decision is ready to make.

Parent Co. is a placeholder. Until the real name exists, it stands in for the parent company brand throughout this document.
This is a working document. It gets updated as the portfolio grows and as we learn. Sections marked in dashes are still being written.

03

Decision framework.

A brand question arrives. We buy a company, we build something we might promote on its own, or something material changes around a brand we already run. The same question gets asked every time. What is this to the portfolio?

The framework answers it the same way every time, no matter who is deciding. One question leads. Everything else gets weighed under it. The answer sets the name, the identity, and the budget.

This is a lens, not a rulebook. The same inputs can point different ways, and a good reason can override any single line.

What serves our customers best?

The customer decides. Everything else is an input we weigh. What do the people who use this actually want, and what would they miss if it changed?

The customer sets the target. The inputs set the path.

Inputs.

Eight inputs, taken in order, one at a time. The numbers are a path, not a ranking. No input carries a fixed weight, because which one matters most changes with the decision, and a scorecard would move the argument from the decision to the math. Work through all of them, and add others when a decision calls for it.

03-01The teamThe people who own what this touches
  • Who owns the areas this decision touches, and what do they know that we do not?
  • Product, engineering, data, payments, finance, marketing, sales, and client success each see a different side of it.
  • Has each of them been asked before the decision is framed, not after?

This one comes first on purpose. Most of the inputs below are answered by these people, and checking with them is a step of its own rather than something scattered across the others. Proposed roles · to confirm

03-02EvidenceWhat the people closest to it are telling us
  • What do our employees tell us?
  • What do our customers tell us?
  • What do the end users tell us?
03-03CategoryThe market it plays in
  • How much category share can this hold?
  • What can it charge?
  • Is the category growing?
03-04TechnologyWhat it is built on
  • Does it run on our existing codebase, or need custom code?
  • What can engineering and product support?
  • Is the way it is built an asset or an obstacle?

A shared platform underneath does not decide a brand. Two brands can run on one codebase, and one brand can run on two. This input tells us what a choice costs, not what the choice is.

03-05ResourcesWhat it takes to run
  • What does it take to run as its own brand?
  • What is the engineering and product upkeep?
  • What does it need from customer support, HR, and finance?
03-06EquityWhat the name is already worth
  • Is there a name customers already know?
  • Do they trust it and search for it?
  • Would they feel the loss if it went away?

Equity is asked per audience. A name can be strong with parents and unknown to buyers, and that difference changes where it belongs.

03-07MarketingWhat it needs to be sold
  • Does it need a unique approach to sales and support?
  • Does it need a standalone website and social presence?
  • Does it appear on its own at industry events?
03-08ReversibilityWhat it costs to change our mind
  • How hard is this to undo if we get it wrong?
  • What does it cost to change our mind later?
  • What does it force us to decide next?
03-??Other inputsAdded as we meet them

Not every decision looks like the ones before it. New considerations get added as we meet them, and the framework makes room.

Traps to avoid.

  • How emotionally attached anyone is to it.
  • How much work went into building it.
  • Who championed it.
  • What we paid to acquire it.
  • What the platform happens to be built on.

None of these tell us what a thing is worth to the portfolio, and all of them are easy to mistake for an answer.

Outputs.

Three answers. We keep the set small so the focus stays on the action, and so brand never becomes a barrier to a decision.

Brand
In the house today Campminder UltraCamp Swimtopia Gazebo

The focus for marketing and public communication. Usually a platform, though it does not have to be. Carries its own name and visual identity, and is sold on its own.

Product
In the house today Campanion under Campminder

Almost always carries its own name, with or without an identity of its own. It can sit under one brand, stretch across several, or stand on its own when it is not big enough to be a brand or to command its own resources.

Feature
In the house today Registration within Campminder Staffing within Campminder Intelligence within Campminder Meet within Swimtopia Ask Camp within Campanion

A capability with a plain, functional name, so you know exactly what it is. Lives entirely inside the brand or platform it belongs to. No public brand of its own.

Where a new acquisition lands.

Four shapes cover almost everything we would buy. Each one runs through the same inputs and comes out somewhere predictable, which is what makes the first conversation short.

03-A
Another camp brand

A company selling to the same camps and camp directors we already serve.

Usually lands as a Brand at first, with the equity and category inputs deciding whether it stays one.

03-B
Something our brands would use

A capability that plugs into what we already sell, in the way Campanion plugs into Campminder.

Usually lands as a Product or a Feature, depending on whether it can be sold on its own.

03-C
A vertical next door

A different audience with the same shape of problem, the way swim sits next to camp.

Lands as its own Brand under Parent Co. It keeps its name because its market is not ours.

03-D
A vertical that does not obviously fit

A business that makes sense to own but does not read as camp or swim from the outside.

Lands as its own Brand, and the work is in the narrative. The story leads with the people it serves and the moments they make, which is the thread every brand here shares, rather than with the category we are known for. Proposed

These shapes also set the communication. A tuck-in for Swimtopia does not get announced the way a whole new vertical does. Section 05 carries a sequence for each.

Before you make the call.

The framework only works if all of it runs. This is the handbook step. Check every box, and an empty box is the work still to do, not a formality to skip.

  • The question led. What serves our customers best was asked first and answered honestly.
  • The team weighed in. Everyone who owns what this touches was asked before the decision was framed, not after. That is input 01.
  • All eight inputs got worked through, in order. The team, evidence, category, technology, resources, equity, marketing, reversibility.
  • No trap did the arguing. Attachment, sunk work, championship, price paid, and platform stayed out of it.
  • The answer is one of three. Brand, Product, or Feature. Anything else means the decision is not done.
  • It is written down. The decision, the date, who made it, and the reasoning go into the playbook file in section 08.
  • The sequence is queued. Who hears it, in what order, starting inside. Section 05 carries it.

Every box checked? Make the call.

04

Narratives.

One story, told the same way by sales, support, product, and leadership. It answers the question before it gets awkward.

TL;DR · The short version

We’re one company behind a few different brands. Every one of them exists for the same reason, to support the people who run camps, teams, and events for young people. We help them make every moment memorable.

04-01

Why Parent Co. exists.

Some people spend their lives building places where a community can gather. Summer camps where kids learn something about themselves. Swim teams where they get to be athletes. It is all the same business, making moments people carry for the rest of their lives. Parent Co. exists because those people deserve tools that are a pleasure to use and that keep getting better every year.

04-02

Why the brands are stronger together.

Parent Co. carries finance, staffing, engineering, and everything no brand should have to solve twice. Every brand runs at full strength because the people behind any one of them are working for all of them.

04-03

Why each brand stands on its own.

Each brand is built for a distinct group of people and answers to them. A camp director and a swim coach do not want the same product and should not be sold the same one. Keeping them separate is what keeps each one specific.

Here’s how the narrative works in action.

Internal
Employees and partners.

We’re here to put big ideas and real skill to work. At Parent Co. we build the teams and the technology behind summer camps, athletic teams, event managers, and more. Every brand here exists to support the people who run them.

External
Trade shows and events.

We’re a growing group of brands guided by a shared set of values, purpose and vision. All of it comes down to supporting the people who run camps, teams, and events for young people. We build the software behind Campminder, UltraCamp, and Swimtopia.

External
Sales conversations.

Every Parent Co. brand is its own platform. They’re built for different customers with different needs, whether that’s camps, teams, events, or something else. I’m here to make sure you get the one that fits how you run.

ExternalProposed
A customer asking what a change means for them.

Your product, your login, and the people you work with stay the same. If anything does change for you, you’ll hear it from us directly, with what it means and when, before you read about it anywhere else.

InternalProposed
People joining us from a company we acquire.

Your brand and your customers are why we wanted you here. The parent carries what no team should have to solve alone, so the name your customers chose keeps running at full strength, now with more behind it.

The two marked proposed are drafts. Written as starting points to react to. Once the language is edited together, the labels come off.
05

Communication sequence.

Concentric circles. The people a change affects most hear about it first, from someone they already know, and every circle out gets a version shaped for what it needs to understand.

Nobody in this sequence should learn about a change from a press release. Roles are named as roles, so the sequence holds as the team changes.

Before any of this runs.

A small group gets pulled in while the decision is still being made, because the answer depends on what they know. Payments, data, engineering, and finance each change the shape of a deal before it is a deal.

Proposed · to confirm against the org chart
  • Finance, from the first conversation. Nothing gets shaped without the numbers.
  • Product and engineering, once a deal is plausible. They say what the platform can carry and what it costs to carry it.
  • Data and payments, before terms are set. Moving customer records and money changes the price of a deal.
  • Marketing and brand, before terms are set, so brand and communication costs sit in the deal model instead of surfacing after close.
  • Legal, throughout.

The decision is made through the framework in section 03 before a single word of this sequence goes out.

The sequence changes with the deal.

A tuck-in that disappears into a product we already sell is not the same announcement as buying into a category nobody expected. Same order, different weight and different reach.

05-A
Another camp brand

All seven steps run in full, and step 05 runs heaviest, because the customers overlap and reps will get asked about both names on the same call. Starts about eight weeks out.

05-B
Something our brands would use

Steps 01 through 04 compress into about a week. Steps 05 and 06 fold into normal product communication, and step 07 is a short announcement rather than a campaign. Starts three to four weeks out.

05-C
A vertical next door

All seven steps, with extra weight on 02 and 04, since a new market has its own language and its own press. The acquired brand keeps its own voice in the external steps. Starts six to eight weeks out.

05-D
A vertical that does not obviously fit

The longest runway. The narrative gets written and tested inside before anything external is scheduled, then all seven steps run in full. Starts about twelve weeks out.

Proposed Starting points to react to, not settled process. The weeks firm up the first time each shape runs.

06

Channels.

Each channel gets its own version of the same story, shaped for how people meet us there. Sales language is the one to protect. Whatever the team says first becomes the story.

How visible is the parent company?

Barely, on purpose. Customers buy Campminder and Swimtopia. The parent carries the story to investors, founders considering a sale, press, and the people we want to hire. On customer-facing brands it reads as a line in the footer, the way you find out a brand belongs to a larger group and then go back to what you were doing.

06-01WebsiteEvery brand, plus the parent
  • Every brand site carries a short line in the footer that it is a Parent Co. company, linking to the parent site.
  • Parent Co. has its own site, aimed at founders, investors, press, and candidates. It never sells software.
  • Careers live on the parent site. If you join any brand here, you join Parent Co.

Proposed Text only. It is quieter, faster to roll out, and easy to upgrade to a lockup later. A logo can wait until the parent has a name and a mark.

06-02EmailCustomers and staff
  • Anything about an acquisition or a brand change comes from the parent company.
  • Day to day email keeps coming from the brand the customer bought.
  • Planning starts the day the decision is made, not the week of the announcement.
06-03New salesThe channel to protect
  • A shared narrative for calls, so every rep answers the portfolio question the same way.
  • A slide in the standard deck, not a new deck.
  • A short answer for “are you getting bought” and “is my product going away.”
06-04Current customersClient success and support
  • Calls for top accounts, email for everyone else, in-person where a visit is already planned.
  • Support gets the same language before the email goes out, because they field the reply.
06-05SocialOwned accounts
  • Review Instagram, LinkedIn, TikTok, Facebook, and Reddit for each brand.
  • Parent Co. likely needs its own accounts, LinkedIn first.
  • An acquired brand’s accounts get a plan, whether that is keeping them, merging them, or retiring them.

Proposed Each brand’s marketing owner runs its accounts day to day. Any post that touches a brand change gets approved by whoever owns marketing at the parent level before it goes out. An acquired brand’s accounts post nothing about the change until the plan for them is set.

06-06PRPress and the wire
  • Press releases are about the parent company acquiring a brand.
  • Sometimes the acquired brand sends its own wire release too.
  • Every acquisition gets something public. Visible and findable beats quiet.
06-07Trade showsWhere the industry sees us
  • Brands show up as brands. The parent is not the thing on the booth.
  • Booth staff get the talking points before they travel.

Proposed Each brand keeps its own show list, owned by its marketing lead, with the camp brands anchored on the national and regional camp conferences and the swim and event brands on their own circuits. Lead times: booth and print to vendors eight weeks out, talking points to booth staff two weeks out.

06-08Collateral and operationsThe unglamorous list
  • Slide decks and sales materials.
  • Merchandise.
  • Checks, invoices, contracts, and payment processing, wherever a company name appears.
  • Email signatures, templates, and anything with a logo on it.
07

Budget.

Percentages, not line items, so the framework holds whether the number is small or large. Set the percentages once a year, then spend against them as things come up.

Two places the money comes from. A standing brand line inside the marketing budget covers the ongoing work. Anything tied to a specific deal comes out of that deal’s budget, where money is already set aside.

Suggested · A starting point to react to

The figures below are a first pass so there is something concrete to argue with. Where a published benchmark exists it is cited. Where one does not, the number is a recommendation and is labeled that way.

Marketing overall

The starting denominator. Private B2B software companies spend a median of 8% of revenue on marketing, and equity-backed companies spend roughly double what bootstrapped ones do.

8%of revenue · median
Standing brand line

Inside the annual marketing budget. Covers the parent brand, naming, brand governance, and keeping this playbook current. There is no reliable public benchmark for brand as a share of marketing spend, so this is ours to set.

10–15%of marketing · proposed
Brand work on a deal

Out of the integration budget rather than marketing. The share depends almost entirely on one thing, whether the name changes. Keeping a name and adding a footer line sits at the bottom. Migrating customers onto a new name sits at the top.

5–20%of integration · proposed
What integration itself runs

For context on the line above. Across 236 deals, median one-time integration cost in technology, media, and telecom came in above 5.6% of the acquired company’s revenue, with rebranding named as one of the cost drivers. Smaller deals run higher as a share, because the fixed costs do not shrink.

5.6%+of target revenue
Ranges, not rates. These set the size of the conversation rather than the size of the check. A specific number comes out of the specific deal.
One thing to avoid. Brand cost is sometimes quoted as a percentage of deal value. No credible published benchmark supports that, and the firms holding the data do not release it. Sizing against the acquired company’s revenue and the integration budget is defensible. Sizing against deal value is not.
Sources. Marketing as a share of revenue, SaaS Capital 2026 Spending Benchmarks for Private B2B SaaS Companies, surveying over 1,000 private B2B SaaS companies. Integration cost, EY-Parthenon 2024 analysis of 236 deals of $500M and above closed between 2010 and 2023. The two proposed figures are Studio Parkside recommendations, not benchmarks.

Where it gets spent.

Phased against the rollout rather than committed as one number, so spend follows the decision instead of leading it.

08

The playbook, in three pieces.

A single source of truth, three ways in. The page is the overview anyone can read in ten minutes. The file is the decision log, and it is the one that gets updated. The skill is how you ask it a question.

Both files are plain text. Nothing to buy, nothing to install, and they open in anything. Version history is the permanent record, so a year from now anyone can see what was decided, when, by whom, and why. Anyone new can run the same framework the same way on their first day.
