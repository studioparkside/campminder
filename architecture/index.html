02-01

The story behind our Parent Co.

How we talk about ourselves.

We’re one company behind a few different brands. Every one of them exists for the same reason, to support the people who run camps, teams, and events for young people. We help them make every moment memorable.

One story, told the same way across our teams.

Public What does Parent Co. do?
The answer

We build the software behind summer camps, swim teams, and the places that host them. One company, several brands, each one made for a specific group of people running programs for young people. If you’ve used Campminder, UltraCamp, or Swimtopia, you’ve already used us.

Sales Why does Parent Co. exist?
The answer

Some people spend their lives building places where a community can gather. Camps where kids learn something about themselves. Swim teams where they get to be athletes. It’s all the same business, making moments people carry for the rest of their lives.

Industry Who works at Parent Co.?
The answer

Engineering, product, support, finance, and operations sit behind every brand in the house rather than inside any one of them. That’s what lets a camp director and a swim coach each get the full weight of the company behind the product they picked.

02-03

Portfolio Tiers

Every asset in our portfolio falls into one of the following tiers.

Brand

The focus for marketing and public communication. Usually a platform, though it does not have to be. Carries its own name and visual identity, and is sold on its own.

Product

Almost always carries its own name, with or without an identity of its own. It can sit under one brand, stretch across several, or stand on its own when it is not big enough to be a brand.

Feature

A capability with a plain, functional name, so you know exactly what it is. Lives entirely inside the brand or platform it belongs to. No public brand of its own.

03

Inputs.

What happens to our portfolio when we buy a company, build something we might promote on its own, or something substantial changes around a brand we already run?

This framework answers it the same way every time, no matter who is deciding.

03Inputs/
03-01

The Inputs

Our customers are our most important inputs. What do the people who use this actually want, and what would they miss if it changed?

Customers

What do the people who use this actually want, and what would they miss if it changed? Everything else on this page is weighed underneath that one question.

Our Team

Did we talk to leadership and every department lead before the decision was framed? Each of them sees a side of this that nobody else in the room can see.

Category

What market does this play in, and how much of it could we hold? What it lets us charge, and whether the whole category is growing or shrinking underneath us.

Technology

What is it built on, and what does that cost us? A shared platform never decides a brand. Two brands can run on one codebase, and one brand can run on two.

Resources

What does it take to actually run? Engineering and product upkeep, the load on support, and the finance and HR time it asks for.

Equity

What is the name already worth? Whether people know it, trust it, and would feel the loss if it went away. A name can be strong with parents and unknown to buyers.

Marketing

What does it take to sell? Whether it needs its own website and social presence, its own approach to sales and support, and its own booth at industry events.

Reversibility

What does it cost to change our mind? How hard this is to undo if we get it wrong, and what saying yes to it forces us to decide next.

04

Outcomes.

Four determinations. Every brand in the house resolves to one of them, weighed against the same inputs. We keep the set small so the focus stays on the action, and so brand never becomes a barrier to a decision.

04Outcomes/
04-01

Answers

What each outcome means in practice.

Keep

The brand stays as it is, with its own name and its own market. Nothing about how it goes to market changes.

Absorb

The brand folds into another brand in the house as a tier, a package, or a feature. The name may survive as a descriptor. The brand does not.

Rebrand

The brand stays distinct and keeps its own market, but its name, logo, visuals, or story shift to connect it to the house, so the relationship is legible from the outside.

Retire

The brand is wound down and its customers are moved onto something else, with a transition story we own rather than one we inherit.

04-02

Watchouts

Four arguments that sound like reasons. But they’re not. Each one has decided a brand question somewhere before, and each one has to stay out of this room.

Attachment

We’re emotionally attached to it. That feeling is real, but it tells us nothing about what the brand is worth to the portfolio or to the people who use it.

Sunk work

How much work went into building it. To get this right we have to ignore effort already spent, because it’s spent either way and it cannot make a weak answer the correct one.

Price paid

What we paid to acquire it. The price settled what we own. It doesn’t get a say in what the brand should become from here.

Platform

What the platform happens to be built on. Two brands can share a codebase and one brand can span two. Architecture tells us what a choice costs, not what the choice is.

05

Sequence.

The order we follow to share any change to the portfolio or one of its brands.

05Sequence/
  1. 01
    Parent Co. board

    The board was part of the decision, so this step confirms it rather than announces it. Nothing moves down the list until that confirmation lands.

  2. 02
    Brand leaders

    Every brand’s leadership, not only the one affected, plus the department leads who will field the questions. Finance, product, engineering, data, payments, legal, marketing, sales, and client success each get the reasoning rather than the announcement.

    Identity and product system

    Name, logo, and how our offerings, products, and features fit together. Finished before all staff see it at step 03.

  3. 03
    All staff

    Town hall and email together. The town hall carries the why and takes questions. The email is the thing people forward and re-read.

  4. 04
    Top accounts

    A call from the person they already work with. Long-standing customers should feel like they were let in, not notified.

    External materials

    Customer email, site updates, sales one-pager, and the answers support works from. Built before step 05.

  5. 05
    Current customers

    Email from the parent company saying what is changing, what is not, and when. If the answer is that nothing changes yet, say that plainly.

    Public presence

    Press release, social accounts and handles, trade show materials. Live at step 06 and not before.

  6. 06
    Everyone else

    Press release, website, social. Public and findable, so it never looks like something we tried to keep quiet.

07

Channels.

Each channel gets its own version of the same story, shaped for how people meet us there.

07Channels/
Website

Every brand site carries a short line in the footer saying it is a Parent Co. company. The parent has its own site aimed at founders, investors, press, and candidates, and it never sells software. Careers live there.

Email

Anything about an acquisition or a brand change comes from the parent company. Everything day to day keeps coming from the brand the customer actually bought.

Sales

One shared answer to the portfolio question, so every rep says the same thing. A slide in the standard deck, or a line in a presentation, shows how each brand and product relates to the parent company, with answers ready for the questions customers ask about where their product is headed.

Social

Each brand’s marketing owner runs its own accounts day to day. Anything touching a brand change gets approved at the parent level before it goes out. The Parent Co. has its own social media presence.

PR

Press releases come from the parent company acquiring a brand, and sometimes the acquired brand sends its own alongside. Every acquisition gets something public. Visible and findable beats quiet.

Events

Brands show up as brands. The parent is not the thing on the booth. Whoever is working the booth gets the talking points before they travel, not after someone asks.

Decks and templates

Slide decks, sales materials, merchandise, email signatures, and templates. Anything with a logo on it gets a pass whenever a name changes.

Contracts and invoices

Checks, invoices, contracts, and payment processing. Anywhere a company name lands in front of a customer, the branding has to match what the customer expects it to say.
