# Campminder Brand Playbook

A working tool, not a slide. This file is instructions for an AI assistant. Load it into a tool like Claude along with `playbook.md`, describe what is on the table, and it walks the steps below with you. It also reads fine as a plain checklist for a human running the framework without one.

This skill reads `playbook.md`, which sits beside it and is the single source of truth for the framework, the figures, and the decisions on record. When this skill and that file disagree, the file wins.

The page is the actions. `playbook.md` is the reasoning. The page tells a reader what each brand is and what to do next, and deliberately does not argue for any of it. So do not treat a claim's absence from the page as evidence it was never reasoned through, and do not reconstruct the reasoning yourself. It is in the file, under "Why each brand sits where it does" and the sections around it.

The two documents agree on every figure. Quote either.

## The question that leads

**What serves our customers best?** The customer sets the target. Every other input sets the path. If an answer cannot be explained in terms of what the customer gets, it is not done.

## Tier and outcome are not the same thing

Keep these apart, because the page keeps them apart and people conflate them constantly.

- **Tier** describes what something *is* today: **Brand**, **Product**, or **Feature**.
- **Outcome** is what this framework *decides*: **Keep**, **Absorb**, **Rebrand**, or **Retire**.

A question answered with a tier is not answered. Push for one of the four outcomes.

**This is a lens, not a rulebook.** The same inputs can point different ways, and a good reason can override any single line. Do not run this as a scoring exercise, and do not tell anyone the framework produced an answer on its own. It organises an argument. People still make the call.

## How to walk a decision

Run these in order. The order is a path, not a ranking, and no input carries a fixed weight.

1. **Name the trigger.** A company we might acquire, something we built that might stand on its own, or something material changing around a brand already in the house. The framework is the same for all three.
2. **Start with customers.** What do the people who use this actually want, and what would they miss if it changed? Ask it of employees, customers, and end users. Every other input is weighed underneath this one.
3. **Check with the team.** Leadership and every department lead who owns what this touches: product, engineering, data, payments, finance, marketing, sales, client success, legal. They get asked before the decision is framed, not after.
4. **Weigh the remaining six.** Category, technology, resources, equity, marketing, reversibility. Equity is asked per audience. A shared codebase never decides a brand on its own. Two brands can run on one codebase, and one brand can run on two. What something is built on tells you what a choice costs, not what the choice is.
5. **Watch the watchouts.** Emotional attachment, sunk work, what was paid, and what the platform is built on. A fifth, who championed it, is off the page for length and still applies. None of these decide anything.
6. **Land on one of four outcomes.** Keep, Absorb, Rebrand, or Retire. Anything else means the decision is not done. Keep is an answer, not a default, and it gets asked again whenever something material changes or at least once a year.
7. **Run the checklist.** Section 04-03 of the playbook page carries it. Six items: customers, our team, other inputs, watchouts avoided, single answer, decision shared. In a meeting, someone reads them aloud and the room confirms each one. When an AI assistant is walking the decision, it asks about each item and names the ones that are not done. Either way, an empty box is work to complete, not a formality.
8. **Record it.** Fill in the decision record template in `playbook.md` and add a one-line entry to the decisions table, in the same edit.
9. **Queue the communication.** Concentric circles, inside first, per section 06 of the page. Board, brand leaders, all staff, top accounts, current customers, everyone else. The acquisition shape picks the variant and the lead time, and those variants live in `playbook.md` rather than on the page.

## What this skill will not do

- It will not attribute a decision to leadership that the decisions table does not record. Report what the table says and who confirmed it.
- It will not size brand cost as a percentage of deal value. No credible benchmark supports that framing.
- It will not skip the customer question, the team check, or the checklist to save time. Those steps are the difference between a decision and an announcement.
- It will not answer a brand question with a tier. Brand, Product, and Feature describe. Keep, Absorb, Rebrand, and Retire decide.
- It will not treat the page as complete. The page is the overview. `playbook.md` carries the acquisition shapes, sequence variants, diligence circle, budget benchmarks, and the reasoning behind each brand's current tier in full.
