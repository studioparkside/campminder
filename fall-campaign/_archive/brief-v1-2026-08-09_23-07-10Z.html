01

The assignment.

Design and build a new homepage for campminder.com, live the week of September 7, alongside the new Registration release.

It works inside the existing site. Same shell, same navigation, same interior pages. This is one page, not a site redesign, and nothing about it should make the rest of campminder.com look like it was left behind.

Everything named on the page has to be live on the platform the day the page goes up. That is the single hard rule and it comes from the company, not from us.

02

Background.

The homepage has not meaningfully changed in four or five years, and the honest reason is that there was never anything new enough to put on it. That changed this fall.

Between September and the end of the year Campminder ships more product than it has in three years. Registration goes generally available September 1. Enrollment reporting, staffing, and an AI assistant inside the Campanion parent app follow. The company is investing again and wants the homepage to work the way Apple's does, showing what is newest rather than explaining what the company is, and changing every few months rather than every few years.

Three pressures shape the page.

A newer competitor is winning on the impression of momentum. They publish constantly, show real product screens, and read as software built for a younger generation of camp directors. Campminder does not need to answer them and will not name them. It needs to stop being abstract.

Campminder has a trust problem of its own making. Things were announced ahead of delivery and customers noticed. The correction is simple and total: only what is live goes on the page.

The commercial focus is net-new business for the first time in several years, with particular attention on camps already paying for another platform. The page has to work for someone comparing, not just someone browsing.

One for all.

One platform for everyone your camp connects to.

Directors fill their camp. Staff get hired and show up ready. Parents register every kid in one pass. Registration, staffing, health, communication, and the business behind it all, with Campminder Intelligence running underneath and a camp success team that has your back.

Take the tour, or we'll walk you through it.

The tagline does not change.

Unpack possible is the existing brand tagline and it stays exactly as it is. Set it the same way every time, in the same lockup, so it reads as one thing rather than a phrase that gets rewritten per page. It sits next to the call to action and it ends the page.

The test
How we will judge it
Two questions
  • Does a camp director understand, in the first screen, that Campminder covers the whole operation?
  • Does the proof arrive before they leave?
If you think the order is wrong, say so.

The order in section 05 is reasoned, and the reasoning is in the section notes, but it has not been tested against a design. You will see things in layout that are invisible in a list. A proposal that moves sections around and explains why is more useful to us than a faithful build of something that turns out not to work.

The one structural principle worth protecting is modularity. Paul wants to evolve this page every three to six months as new product ships, so blocks need to come apart, reorder, and be replaced without a rebuild.

07

What we want.

Less a look than a set of behaviours. Where these conflict with each other, the earlier one wins.

01
Show the product. Do not describe it.

Real screens from the real platform, current as of launch. The current site says Campminder has twenty-plus camp-specific tools and shows almost none of them. Every claim on the new page should have something visible sitting next to it.

02
Motion only where a task completes.

Two moments, not a page that moves everywhere. The useful kind shows something getting done: a form filling, a dashboard assembling, a placement resolving. Decoration that moves is worse than stillness.

03
Real people, from real camps.

Photography from actual customer camps and actual Campminder staff. No stock, no models, no rows of headshots. This is a business where the customers know each other, and anything staged will be recognised as staged.

04
One moment of real density.

The coverage section. Most software homepages are a sequence of generous, airy sections that each say one thing. Somewhere on this page there should be a block that is dense on purpose, where the sheer amount of it is the message.

05
Editorial structure over marketing-page structure.

Numbered rails, stacked rows with hairline dividers, oversized headlines carrying their own sections, pill labels used sparingly on data rather than as decoration. What we would rather avoid is the default of a tracked-out small-caps eyebrow above every headline and three even columns under it.

06
Plain language, in front.

No acronyms, no API or webhook talk, nothing a camp director has to decode. Technical language earns a place only where someone can act on it. The audience is proud of being camp people rather than technology people.

07
It has to belong to the site it is on.

The rest of campminder.com is not being redesigned. The new homepage should read as the most current page on the site, not as a page from a different site.

08

Guardrails.

Set with Campminder's leadership, sales, product, and client success teams in the working session on August 3. These are not preferences. Each one exists because of something specific that happened.

01
No pain-point selling.

We do not remind directors that their job is hard. They know. After fifteen years of writing that way, the company's own sales lead called it condescending. We show what they get instead.

02
No future tense.

If it is not live, it is not on the page. No coming soon, no roadmap, no dates. Announcing ahead of delivery is what damaged trust in the first place, and this page is part of the repair.

03
No speed claims.

Nothing about shipping faster, innovating faster, or moving quickly. Speed gets proven by a visible release record and then shown, not asserted first.

04
No running against anyone.

No competitor named, referenced, or answered. No comparison tables, no versus pages, no implied contrast. Campminder states what it stands for and lets the customer draw the conclusion.

05
No tech talk without a use.

Modern under the hood, plain on the page. Technical language earns its place only when a camp director can act on it.

09-03 Dates and dependencies

September 1Fixed
Registration goes generally available.

The product milestone the page depends on. A long-form announcement publishes the same day, and the homepage links to it rather than repeating it.

September 7Target
The homepage goes live.

Labor Day week, after the announcement, so the announcement has somewhere to send people. This date moves only if a named feature is not live.

OngoingAfter launch
The page keeps changing.

Every three to six months as new product ships. Build it so a block can be swapped, reordered, or added by Campminder's team without coming back to us.

09-04 Not in this scope

Navigation and information architecture. Interior pages. Full page copy beyond headlines and section messaging. Photography, video, and illustration production. Anything on the site other than this one page.

One review before anything goes live.

Weekly working sessions through design and build, then a brand and design review before release. Feedback runs on a one business day turnaround in both directions.

If something hits a wall, on design or on copy, raise it early rather than solving around it. Most of the constraints in this brief exist for a reason, and the ones that do not should be identified and dropped.
