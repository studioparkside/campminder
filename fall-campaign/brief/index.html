01

Assignment.

A new homepage for campminder.com, live the week of September 7, alongside the Registration release.

It works inside the existing site. Same shell, same navigation, same interior pages. One page, not a redesign.

Everything named on the page has to be live the day it goes up. That's the one hard rule, and it comes from Campminder.

02

Background.

02-01 Why now

The homepage hasn't meaningfully changed in five years, because there was never anything new enough to put on it. That changed this fall. Campminder ships more product between September and December than it has in three years, starting with Registration on September 1, then Campminder Intelligence, staffing, and Ask Camp, the assistant inside the Campanion parent app.

Campminder Intelligence runs across the whole platform. Registration, staffing, health, and finance in one view. It isn't an enrollment report and it shouldn't be presented as one.

02-02 Pressure

Momentum. A newer competitor publishes constantly and reads as software built for a younger generation of camp directors. Campminder doesn't need to answer them. It needs to stop being abstract.

Trust. Campminder announced things ahead of delivery and customers noticed. Only what's live goes on the page.

New business. The commercial focus is net-new for the first time in years, with attention on camps already paying for something else.

02-03 Who lands here

Camps shopping for a platform, and existing customers on their way to log in. The page works for both or it doesn't work.

02-04 The current page

07

UX requests.

Behaviours, not a look. Where they conflict, the earlier one wins.

01
Show the product.

Real screens, current at launch. Every claim should have something visible sitting next to it.

02
Motion where a task completes.

Two moments, not a page that moves. A form filling, a dashboard assembling. Decoration that moves is worse than stillness.

03
Real people, real camps.

Customer camps and actual Campminder staff. No stock, no headshot grids. These customers know each other and they'll spot anything staged.

04
One dense moment.

Somewhere on the page, a block where the sheer amount of it is the message. Everything else can breathe.

05
Editorial, not marketing page.

Numbered rails, stacked rows with hairline dividers, oversized headlines carrying their sections. Not a tracked small-caps eyebrow over every headline with three even columns underneath.

06
Plain language.

No acronyms, nothing to decode. This audience is proud of being camp people rather than technology people.

07
It belongs to the site it's on.

The most current page on campminder.com, not a page from a different site.

08

Guardrails.

Set with Campminder's leadership, sales, product, and client success on August 3. Each one exists because of something that happened.

01
No pain-point selling.

We don't remind directors that their job is hard. They know. We show what they get.

02
No future tense.

If it isn't live, it isn't on the page. No coming soon, no roadmap, no dates.

03
No speed claims.

Speed gets proven by a visible release record, then shown. Not asserted first.

04
No running against anyone.

No competitor named, referenced, or answered. No comparison tables, no versus pages.

05
No tech talk without a use.

Modern under the hood, plain on the page. Technical language earns its place only when a camp director can act on it.
