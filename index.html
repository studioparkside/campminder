02

A House of Brands.

  • Campminder stays Campminder.
  • The new Parent Co. company holds the portfolio and is the umbrella organization for all of our offerings.
  • Every brand keeps its own name and speaks to its own market.

The rest of this page is how we keep that straight.

One parent. Independent brands.

In a House of Brands, the parent company stands behind fully independent brands. Customers buy from the name they know and trust. The parent signals shared ownership, shared talent, and shared ambition. Examples include PepsiCo and Meta.

Here’s why this is the right model for Campminder.

02-01

Campminder has real equity.

Customers keep the name they trust. Parent Co. adds a layer above Campminder instead of replacing it.

02-02

The brands serve different markets.

A Campminder customer and a Swimtopia customer have different needs and different reasons to buy. Each brand speaks to its own market without compromise.

02-03

The company already works this way.

Shared engineering, shared finance, and shared leadership already run beneath multiple product names. The model gives that structure a name and makes it visible.

04

Decision framework.

Something new arrives. We buy a company, or we build something we might promote on its own. The same question gets asked every time. What is this to the portfolio?

The framework below answers it the same way every time, no matter who is deciding. One question leads. Everything else gets weighed under it. The answer sets the name, the identity, and the budget.

This is a lens, not a rulebook. The same inputs can point different ways, and a real reason can override any single line.

It stays simple enough to act on, and every choice here can be explained, not just made.

What serves our customers best?

The customer decides. Everything else is an input we weigh. What do the people who use this actually want, and what would they miss if it changed?

Each input shapes the answer, and none of them makes the call alone. A build that is easy but that no customer asked for is not a reason to ship it. A name that is awkward but that customers know is not a reason to drop it.

The customer sets the target. The inputs set the path.

Inputs.

These are the inputs we weigh when a new brand action hits. We look through all of them, weigh each on its own, and add others when a decision calls for it.

04-01
Evidence
  • What do our employees tell us?
  • What do our customers tell us?
  • What do the end users tell us?
04-02
Category
  • How much category share can this hold?
  • What can it charge?
  • Is the category growing?
04-03
Technology
  • Does it run on our existing codebase, or need custom code?
  • What can engineering and product support?
  • Is the way it is built an asset or an obstacle?
04-04
Resources
  • What does it take to run as its own brand?
  • What is the engineering and product upkeep?
  • What does it need from customer support, HR, and finance?
04-05
Equity
  • Is there a name customers already know?
  • Do they trust it and search for it?
  • Would they feel the loss if it went away?
04-06
Marketing
  • Does it need a unique approach to sales and support?
  • Does it need a standalone website and social presence?
  • Does it appear on its own at industry events?
04-07
Reversibility
  • How hard is this to undo if we get it wrong?
  • What does it cost to change our mind later?
  • What does it force us to decide next?
04-??
Other inputs

Not every decision looks like the ones before it. New considerations get added as we meet them, and the framework makes room.

The framework adjusts as our parent brand and internal team grow and learn.

Outputs.

A brand architecture framework could conceivably carry more options than this. A good one is right-sized for the company it serves and built to change as that company grows. We keep ours simple so the focus stays on the action, and so brand never becomes a barrier to innovation or a decision.

Brand
In the house today Campminder UltraCamp Swimtopia Gazebo

The focus for marketing and public communication. Usually a platform, though it does not have to be. Carries its own name and visual identity, and is sold on its own.

Product
In the house today Campanion under Campminder Meet Maestro under Swimtopia

Almost always carries its own name, with or without an identity of its own. It can sit under one brand, stretch across several, or stand on its own when it is not big enough to be a brand or to command its own resources.

Feature
In the house today Registration within Campminder Staffing within Campminder Intelligence within Campminder Ask Camp within Campanion

A capability with a plain, functional name, so you know exactly what it is. Lives entirely inside the brand or platform it belongs to. No public brand of its own.

Traps to avoid.

  • How emotionally attached anyone is to it.
  • How much work went into building it.
  • Who championed it.
  • What we paid to acquire it.

None of these tell us what a thing is worth to the portfolio, and all of them are easy to mistake for an answer.

05

Brand narratives.

One story, told the same way by sales, support, product, and leadership. It answers the question before it gets awkward. Three parts, and they go in this order.

TL;DR · The Short Version

We’re one company behind a few different brands. Every one of them exists for the same reason, to support the people who run camps, teams, and events for young people. We help them make every moment memorable.

05-01

Why Parent Co. exists.

Some people spend their lives building places where a community can gather. Summer camps where kids learn something about themselves. Swim teams where they get to be athletes. It is all the same business, making moments people carry for the rest of their lives. Parent Co. exists because those people deserve tools that are a pleasure to use and that keep getting better every year. We are here so they can make every moment memorable.

05-02

Why the brands are stronger together.

Parent Co. carries finance, staffing, engineering, and everything no brand should have to solve twice. Every brand runs at full strength because the people behind any one of them are working for all of them.

05-03

Why each brand stands on its own.

Each brand is built for a distinct group of people and answers to them. A camp director and a swim coach do not want the same product and should not be sold the same one. Keeping them separate is what keeps each one specific. Every brand is remarkable on its own terms and a real piece of a system thought all the way through.

Here’s how the narrative works in action.

Internal
Employees and partners.

We’re here to put big ideas and real skill to work. At Parent Co. we build the teams and the technology behind summer camps, athletic teams, event managers, and more. Every brand here exists to support the people who run them.

External
Trade shows and events.

We’re a growing group of brands guided by a shared set of values, purpose and vision. All of it comes down to supporting the people who run camps, teams, and events for young people. We build the software behind Campminder, UltraCamp, and Swimtopia.

External
Sales conversations.

Every Parent Co. brand is its own platform. They’re built for different customers with different needs, whether that’s camps, teams, events, or something else. I’m here to make sure you get the one that fits how you run.
